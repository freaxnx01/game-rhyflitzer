The game shows these sources on its start screen.

Road flag `modern` (Swiss side and Fridolinsbrücke): set by `pipeline/era_roads.py` on roads that did not exist in 1952 (motorways, the Fridolinsbrücke, minor roads in the Sisslerfeld industrial site, residential, living_street and service ways without an old house within 25 m; a cut-off end gets its access back only along the shortest route (see `keep1952`)). The site polygon `pipeline/industrial_sisslerfeld.geojson` is OSM way w1378060195 © OpenStreetMap contributors (ODbL). Road key `keep1952: [[a, b], ...]` (sorted intervals in metres along `pts`): a residential street keeps its house stretch (first old house minus 10 m to last plus 10 m; a house counts for its nearest street only) plus the shortest route from each such stretch, and from each cut-off island of kept roads, to the old network (the largest Swiss connected component of the kept roads that are not residential streets; every other such component is joined by a route too); hidden streets on a route keep only the route metres, and a residential street with no footprint of its own and no route is hidden; the game hides the rest as `modern`. Rules and CLI: `docs/11-pipeline-osm.md`. It also sets `modern: true` on the landmark anchor `fridolinsbruecke` when its road is hidden (`era_fields.py` leaves that anchor alone), so the bridge's viewpoints and J entry are not offered in 1952.

Traced houses of 1952 (#530): `world.era.houses` (written by `pipeline/era_traced.py`, after `era_1952.py` and before `era_roads.py`) is a list of buildings of the same shape `osmBuilding` reads for `buildings` (`id` `sisseln-NN`, `h` 6, `roof` `gable`, `palette` `village`, `rect`, `ring`, all in game coordinates) for houses that stood in Sisseln in 1946-1952 and are gone today. They were traced by hand from the Siegfried map and SWISSIMAGE HIST 1946 © swisstopo (tracing reference only, no imagery shipped); `era` keeps `houses` as its last key. Rules and CLI: `docs/11-pipeline-osm.md`.

Field strips of 1952 and site flags (#530): `world.era.fields` (written by `pipeline/era_fields.py`, after `era_1952.py` and `era_roads.py`) is a list of entries shaped like `fields` items (`kind`, `ring`, optional `holes`, `dir`, `src: "era"`) that cover the Sisslerfeld industrial site except where old roads, rail, streams, forest, water, today's fields, old buildings and car parks stay. The same step sets `modern: true` (last key) on the `parking` lots, `props` and `anchors.landmarks` entries inside the site (the DSM chimney and water tower; `plattform` is never flagged) and removes it elsewhere; the game hides them in the 1952 layer. Rules and CLI: `docs/11-pipeline-osm.md`.

Swiss bridge and underpass axes (#1151): `pipeline/tlm_structures.json.gz` (5.1 MB, format `TLMS1`) is not a world file but a CH-wide pipeline input, committed next to the script that cuts it, like `pipeline/ch_outline.geojson`. It holds the 3D axis of every swissTLM3D road and rail record with a real `KUNSTBAUTE` (bridge, underpass, tunnel, gallery, ford, dam — 68 790 road and 8 544 rail records in release 2026-02), plus the 21 563 records that cross one, clipped to the 40 m around it: those carry what passes *under* a bridge, which swissTLM3D files as `KUNSTBAUTE = Keine`. Coordinates are absolute LV95 metres (E/N to 0.1 m, Z to 0.01 m). Source: swissTLM3D © swisstopo (Open Government Data, free use, attribution required). Rebuild it per TLM release with `python pipeline/tlm_structures.py --release <YYYY-MM>`; the 3.6 GB release zip is streamed into a temp directory and deleted again, never kept in the repo.

Measured bridge heights (#1151): the keys `tlm` and `tlmUnder` (written by `pipeline/world_tlm.py` from `tlm_structures.json.gz`, on every rail deck in `railBridges` and every road bridge a swissTLM3D bridge axis matches at 8 m / 25 deg over half its length; hero bridges never get them). `tlm` is the deck's height profile `[[s, y], ...]` (arc length along `pts`, game metres every 2 m; for rail the top of the rail, the game subtracts 0.3 m for the deck surface). `tlmUnder` is what passes under the deck `[[x, z, y, kind], ...]` (kind `road` or `rail`, game coordinates): the game sets the cut floor of that crossing from it. Without `tlm` a deck behaves as before. Heights: swissTLM3D © swisstopo (Open Government Data, free use, attribution required); game y = Z - the terrain's base.

Road height profiles (#1152): road key `zp` (written by `pipeline/road_profiles.py`, idempotent, also by `osm.py build --profiles` and by every editor world) is the road's own smooth height profile — heights in game metres (absolute minus the terrain's base) every 4 m of arc length along `pts`, the last at the road's full length. It comes from the swissALTI3D 2 m terrain along the road axis, Gaussian-smoothed by road class and pinned at junctions, so a road no longer picks up the embankments and ditches beside it from the 16 m terrain mesh. The game lays the road ribbon on it and fits the terrain at the road edges to it. Source: swissALTI3D © swisstopo. Rules and CLI: `docs/11-pipeline-osm.md`.
